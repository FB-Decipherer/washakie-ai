GYR-O Pages and the UW AI Initiative

Where this starts: the Governor’s Executive Order

On June 3, 2026, Governor Mark Gordon signed Executive Order 2026-03, directing state agencies to promote responsible development of data centers, the facilities AI runs inside. It calls for workforce training with the University of Wyoming and for real benefits to the communities that host them.

Washakie County will host no data center and no AI researchers. GYR-O Pages answers the other half of the question: how does AI reach the grass roots?

Source: Cowboy State Daily, June 3, 2026

One of the UW President’s Initiatives: the AI Initiative, in its own words

“The University of Wyoming’s AI Initiative is a bold, people-centered effort to shape the future of our state, empowering citizens and communities to thrive in an AI-driven world. By addressing key industries like agriculture, engineering, energy, tourism, wildlife conservation, and rural healthcare, UW is ensuring that AI enriches lives and drives sustainable growth. This initiative will enhance the University of Wyoming’s ability to bring advances in AI to disciplines across the university to advance the state. It will attract investments, build corporate partnerships, seed entrepreneurship, and equip every student and community to participate in the global AI transformation, securing a prosperous future for all of Wyoming.”

Source: uwyo.edu › Office of the President › Initiatives › AI Initiative

Why GYR-O Pages fits

  • “People-centered”The owner describes what they want in their own words, and AI builds it.
  • “Empowering citizens and communities”No training, no software, almost no cost. The site is theirs outright.
  • “Seed entrepreneurship”Farms, shops and small offices get a working website of their own.
  • “Agriculture”Farm sites, shared under a farm cooperative’s name.
  • “Equip every student and community”Public workshops, with high school ambassadors doing the demonstrations.
  • “All of Wyoming”Built for a county of 7,600, far from Laramie and Cheyenne.
  • Ready nowNine sites running. A 12-month Washakie County pilot for about $10,000.
