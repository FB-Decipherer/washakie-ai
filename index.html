What this is

A $10,000 request to the Wyoming Business Council for a twelve-month pilot: twelve working websites for Washakie County subjects, four public workshops, and a method published free.

A  Plain answers

Start here. No jargon, nothing assumed. If you read only this column you will know what is being asked for, what it buys, and who it is for.

What is being asked for?

Ten thousand dollars. One award, one time, from the Wyoming Business Council’s Community Economic Growth Grant1 — a program that exists to fund projects improving a community’s quality of life through economic development, and whose ceiling is exactly $10,000.

It pays for a twelve-month pilot in Washakie County: twelve working websites built for local institutions and small businesses, four public workshops, and a written method anybody can follow afterward without paying anyone.

That is the whole ask. There is no second phase, no ongoing subsidy, and nothing to renew.

Why does Washakie County need this?

Because the state’s artificial-intelligence money has an address, and it is not here.

In January 2026 the Laramie County Commission approved a 1.8 gigawatt data center outside Cheyenne — six hundred acres of building and another six hundred and fifty-nine for the power plant that feeds it, four hundred permanent jobs when it is finished2. The University of Wyoming runs the state’s AI initiative from Laramie, with roughly four million dollars of federal money for computing hardware3.

In June 2026 the Governor signed an executive order setting the state’s terms for this industry — water, power, permits, wildlife, and a workforce pipeline through the colleges9. Every clause of it is about facilities the size of a power station, which is the right concern for a gigawatt.

Both are real, and neither is a complaint. But a county of about 7,600 people, whose population has been falling for twenty-five years4, does not get a data center and does not get a research center. It gets whatever it builds for itself.

Wyoming is building the engine room. Nobody is teaching the rest of the state to drive.

What does the money actually buy?

Twelve websites, four workshops, and a method left behind in public.

  • Twelve sites for Washakie County subjects — a county office, the library, the fair board, a rancher, a welder, a bookkeeper, whoever comes forward. One topic each, built to the same format.
  • Four public workshops, open to anyone, where the work is done in the room rather than described.
  • A written method, published free, so the thirteenth site costs nobody anything.

Each page is one HTML file — no framework, no build step, nothing to install — and a site is that page plus a few small things beside it: an icon, a social card, a list of its own links. There is no database and no server. It runs for about a dollar a month in domain registration and nothing else7, and the person it belongs to owns it outright.

What is a Three Columns site?

A page built so three different visitors each get what they came for without wading through the other two. Plain answers on the left. Every source previewed in the middle. Full technical detail on the right. On a phone the three become three buttons; printed, they become one document.

The middle column is the point. When a page claims something, the claim is attached to a card naming the source, who made it, and what you would find there — so a stranger can check it in ten seconds instead of taking it on faith.

You are reading one now. This page is the proposal and the sample.

Where is the proof it works?

worland-dog-law.online5 is a finished instance, built before any grant money existed. It takes the Worland municipal dog ordinances, sets them out in plain language, links every claim to the ordinance text, and adds an audit of where the published code and the real code disagree.

It was built for the cost of a domain name. It is the argument for the whole pilot: this is not a proposal to find out whether the idea works.

Who does the work?

Walter Wilkinson, of Worland. Retired technology engineer, systems engineering degree from Boston University, about twenty years in the Boston software industry before Wyoming.

The format6 and the working instance were both built here, unpaid, before this application existed.

What happens if the grant is not awarded?

The work continues, slower and smaller. The format is already built, already published and already free. Nothing here depends on the money.

What the money buys is pace and reach — twelve subjects in a year instead of two, workshops instead of word of mouth, and a documented method instead of one person’s habit. That is a fair thing for a grant to buy, and an honest thing to say out loud.

What is the catch?

Two, stated plainly.

Somebody has to apply. The program funds communities and organizations, not individuals, so a local body has to be the applicant — the city, the county, the library, or an economic development organization. Finding that sponsor is the first task, not an afterthought.

Twelve subjects have to want one. A site nobody asked for is a site nobody maintains. The pilot is built around subjects who come forward, and the number is a target rather than a promise.

C  How it works

Concepts first, mechanics last. A reader who stops a third of the way down should still have learned something whole.

The shape of the pilot

Twelve months, twelve sites, four workshops, one published method. The unit of work is a single subject — one office, one ordinance, one trade, one question — and the finished page is one HTML file of about fifty kilobytes, styles and scripts inside it, that renders before a loading spinner would have appeared. Around that page sit an icon, a social card and two or three short text files; a subject that needs more than one page gets more than one page, as worland-dog-law.online does5.

Nothing in the pilot creates an ongoing obligation for the county. There is no platform to subscribe to, no account to keep, no vendor to be locked into, and no second year of funding contemplated.

Where the money goes

Ten thousand dollars, allocated before the application rather than after the award1:

12 sites @ $500            $6,000
4 public workshops         $1,600
Method, guides, handover   $1,000
Domains, 12 x 2 years        $360
Materials and rooms          $400
Contingency                  $640
                        ---------
                          $10,000

The $500 per site is production, not software: reading the source material, writing the plain-language column, building and verifying every source card, and sitting down with the subject to hand it over. The technology is free7. The work is the cost.

Who applies, and why that is the first problem

The program funds communities and organizations. An individual cannot be the applicant, so the pilot needs a local sponsor to carry it — realistically the City of Worland, Washakie County, the county library, or a local economic development organization. That conversation has to happen before the paperwork, not alongside it.

The sponsor is not being asked for money or staff time. It is being asked to hold the grant and receive the deliverables, which are public artifacts it would own outright at the end.

The calendar

  • Now — confirm the grant cycle by telephone; find and brief a sponsoring organization; assemble a first list of willing subjects.
  • 1 March — application deadline, next cycle.
  • Months 1–3 — workshop one; first three sites.
  • Months 4–9 — workshops two and three; six more sites.
  • Months 10–12 — workshop four; final three sites; method published and handed over.

The 1 September deadline for the current year has passed. The next is 1 March, which is a schedule rather than a delay: it leaves time to secure a sponsor and line up subjects, and a pilot that arrives with twelve willing participants already named is a materially stronger application than one that promises to find them.

How anyone would know whether it worked

Stated in advance, because a measure invented afterward is not a measure:

  • Sites live and reachable twelve months after the award, each on its own domain, each owned by its subject.
  • Sites still standing six months after that, without further intervention — the real test of a thing that costs a dollar a month to keep.
  • Attendance at the four workshops, counted honestly, including the ones nobody comes to.
  • A thirteenth site built by somebody else, from the published method, without asking for help. That is the only measure that matters, and the only one that cannot be manufactured.

What the county owns at the end

Twelve working sites, held by their subjects. A written method in the public domain. Twelve domain names paid two years forward. And a demonstration, in a county of 7,600 people4, that the useful end of this technology is not a building full of servers — it is a rancher with a page that proves what he says.

Why this is cheap

Because none of the expensive parts are being bought. The layout already exists and is published free6. The hosting is free for public repositories7. The certificates are free and automatic. The fonts are open-licensed. A finished site has no database, no backend, no login and no dependency that can be withdrawn.

What remains is the labor of reading a source carefully and writing it down honestly, and that is the part AI genuinely accelerates — not by inventing content, but by doing in an afternoon the reading and drafting that used to take a fortnight, with every claim still traceable to the document it came from.

Risks, stated plainly

  • No sponsor comes forward. The most likely failure, and the earliest. Mitigation is to ask early and ask several.
  • Fewer than twelve subjects volunteer. Then fewer sites are built and the unspent portion is returned. A padded count would be worse than a short one.
  • The program is closed or changed. The grant page carries a stale reopening notice1, which is exactly why it gets confirmed by telephone before anything else is done.
  • The sites are built and then ignored. Possible. It is why the six-month survival check is in the measures above, and why subjects are asked to come forward rather than being recruited.

This proposal is written in the format it proposes to teach. Every figure in column A is attached to a card in column B naming where it came from. That is not a stylistic choice — it is the argument.
